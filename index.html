    Good to know

    Why check a checksum?

    A checksum is a fingerprint of a file. If even one byte differs, the fingerprint changes completely. Checking it tells you the download was not damaged and is the file the publisher put the checksum next to.

    Which one should I use?

    Use what the download page gives, preferably SHA-256 or SHA-512. MD5 and SHA-1 still catch damaged downloads, but someone determined can make two different files with the same MD5 or SHA-1, so do not rely on them against tampering. CRC32 is the quick check zip and gzip use.

    Is my file sent anywhere?

    No. Verity reads the file in pieces on this page and computes the checksums in WebAssembly, in your browser. It makes no network request with your file or your checksums, and it stores nothing but your theme choice.

    Verity is open source (MIT). The checksums are computed by a 6 KB WebAssembly module written in AssemblyScript.
